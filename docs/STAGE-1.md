## A. Diagnosis of the current site and model

**The site sells one free thing and three unpriced things.** Every paid step (Audit, Foundation, Retainer) says "quoted case by case, no public price list." That line appears on nearly every page. An owner reading it on their phone learns there is a free check, and that everything after it is a mystery. That is the opposite of easy to buy.

**Too many steps before money changes hands.** Snapshot → Audit → Foundation → Retainer is four decisions. Each one is a chance to drift off. The Audit is a written plan; owners rarely want to pay for a plan. They want the thing fixed.

**The language is for SEO people, not owners.** "Hire-intent prompts", "mention / cite / none", "GEO", "extractable answers on money pages", "buy-ladder", "Clarity path". A plumber will not say any of that out loud.

**AI is the headline. It should be included, not the headline.** Leading with ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity makes the offer feel speculative and pushes close to a promise nobody can keep. Local jobs are still decided mostly on Google and Google Maps. The same work helps all three, so say that plainly.

**Defensive tone.** The sample "Coastal Pools SEQ" readout is repeated on three pages and spends most of its words explaining what it is not. A lot of "what this isn't", not much "here is what you get, here is what it costs."

**One half-specialisation.** A single industry page (pools and landscape) and a vague "local businesses" elsewhere.

**Small things.** No real form (contact is a mailto link). Autoplay hero video. Dark navy everywhere makes long text harder to read on a phone.

**What is good and worth keeping.** Honest: no fake proof. Solo is stated clearly. Focus on jobs, not vanity metrics. Google + Maps + AI as the three places buyers look. The name, the warm copper accent, and the Plex serif/sans type.

## B. The new model

**Who it is for.** Owner-run local service businesses anywhere in Australia (Gold Coast based, SEQ is home turf, all work remote) that get work when someone nearby searches, compares two or three options, and calls or books. Trades and home services first (plumbers, electricians, builders, pool and landscape, pest, air con, cleaners, arborists). Clinics second (physio, dental, chiro, vets, podiatry). Local professionals where it fits. Not online shops, national brands, or anyone who wants ads or social media.

**What it sells.** One free first step and four fixed-price packages. Each package is a fixed list of work. When the list is done, the job is done. No time windows are sold; a finish date is given in writing before work starts, and if it's missed the final payment isn't due until the list is complete.

| | Price | What you get |
|---|---|---|

Prices in AUD plus GST. Second location Tune-Up $390. If a Fix-Up site turns out to be unfixable at the plan stage, the client pays $490 for the plan and Tune-Up, keeps both, and that $490 comes off a New Website.

**How people buy.** Form on the site → free check by email within two business days → book a package straight from its page (form, no payment taken) or call. Alexander does the work. Weekly Friday email while work is on. Client owns every account, page and domain.

**Why this is better than the current model.**
- Five things on the page, all priced. An owner can point at one.
- One decision between free and paid, not three.
- The $990 "written plan" becomes a $490 job that gets done.
- Nothing is sold on time. Everything is sold on a list of work, which is what the owner actually gets.
- Website builds are on the menu, so the most common "your site can't be fixed" outcome has an answer instead of a referral.
- AI becomes an included check and a benefit of the same work, not a headline promise.
- Language shifts to calls, bookings and jobs. Every SEO term is explained in the next sentence.
- Solo is the pitch: you deal with the person doing the work.
- Proof is a checklist and a monthly report, not adjectives. Client names get added only with permission.

## C2. Funnel, CRM and SOPs

**Funnel** (`FUNNEL.md`): nine stages from New lead to Monthly/Dormant/Lost. Fixed rhythm: free check in 2 business days, follow-up at 7 days, nudge at 30, dormant with a quarterly check-in at 90. Three touches maximum. Every stage change creates the next task and logs the email template used.

**CRM preview** (`crm/index.html`): Dashboard (alerts, KPIs, tasks due, Friday updates due, capacity vs 25h/week) · Pipeline board with drag-and-drop · Contacts · Contact page with timeline, tasks, jobs · Free-check builder that writes the T2 email and moves the stage · Jobs with the package checklists, invoices, finish date and Friday log · Monthly with the month's cycle and a report builder that writes T12 · Templates filled for any contact · Playbook (SOP index and the checklists) · Settings with export/import. "Simulate website form" shows what happens when a lead lands.

**SOPs** (`sops/`): 01 sales and funnel (lead handling, free check, follow-up, booking) · 02 onboarding and delivery (onboarding, Tune-Up, Fix-Up, New Website, Monthly cycle) · 03 communication and reporting (Friday update, reports, handover, reviews and permission) · 04 money (invoicing, late payment, refunds, the finish-date rule) · 05 operations (capacity and finish dates, QA, tools and security, when things go wrong, weekly and monthly review) · 06 own marketing · 07 templates (T1–T15, agreement, partner one-pager, report layouts).

**Stage 2 recommendation:** build the admin into the existing Next.js site with Supabase and Resend, invoices from Xero or MYOB by bank transfer. Small: about 10 tables, 8 routes, 9 screens. Or run the preview in a browser for the first clients and build once it's earning.
