# 05 — Operations

## SOP-15 Capacity and finish dates

**Weekly capacity:** [confirm] billable hours a week. Default in the CRM: 25. The rest is sales, admin, your own marketing, and learning.

**Hours per package (for planning, not for billing)**
- Free check: 0.75 h
- Profile Tune-Up: 6 h
- Website Fix-Up: 20 h
- New Website: 40 h
- Monthly Steady: 4 h/month · Growth: 8 h/month

**Working out a finish date**
1. Add up hours already committed this week and next (the CRM shows it).
2. Find the week where the new job's hours fit without going over capacity.
3. Add a buffer: Tune-Up +3 business days, Fix-Up +5, New Website +10. Client delays are the usual cause and the buffer absorbs the small ones.
4. Add 5 business days for onboarding (access, photos, answers).
5. That's the date. Round to a Friday. Write it in the booking confirmation and the job.

**Rules**
- Never more than 2 New Websites or 3 Fix-Ups active at once. If you're full, tell the lead the real start date. People wait for honest.
- Say no to work that doesn't fit the packages. It eats the week and never gets finished.
- Monthly clients' hours come first each month. They're the base.

## SOP-16 Quality check before anything goes live

**When:** before any page, profile change or report goes to a client or public. Ten minutes. On a phone, not a laptop.

**Checklist (CRM: "QA")**
1. Read it out loud. Anything you stumble on gets rewritten.
2. Does the first line say what and where?
3. Is every fact right: business name spelled as the client spells it, phone, suburbs, hours, prices, ABN?
4. Any word from the banned list? (unlock, elevate, leverage, cutting-edge, dominate, synergy, holistic, best-in-class, guaranteed, page one, "we pride ourselves"). Remove.
5. Any claim you can't back? ("Gold Coast's leading…", "5-star rated" without 5 stars). Remove.
6. Phone number taps to call. Form submits and arrives. Links go where they say.
7. Photos are real, load fast, and have alt text.
8. Page title and description set and different from other pages.
9. Looks right on a phone: no tiny text, no sideways scroll, button reachable with a thumb.
10. Nothing is left as "lorem", "TODO", "[placeholder]" or a template default.

**Done when:** all ten pass. If any fail, fix and re-run the failed ones.

- Design and screenshots: whatever's quick. Loom for handover videos.
- Booking link: Cal.com (free) for 20-minute calls.

**Access rules**
1. Two-factor on everything of yours. No exceptions.
2. Client accounts stay in the client's name. You're a manager or delegate. If a client insists on giving you their password, store it in the password manager, use it, and get them to change it when the job ends.
3. Never send a password by email or text. Password manager share links, or a phone call.
4. Client folders in the password manager are archived on offboarding (SOP-12). Nothing kept.
5. Business laptop: disk encryption on, screen lock, backups running. If it's lost, every password gets rotated the same day.
6. Suspect a breach (odd login alert, client says something changed they didn't do): change the affected passwords, remove unknown managers from the profile, tell the client the same day in plain words. Write down what happened.

## SOP-19 When things go wrong

**A finish date is at risk**
1. Say it the moment you know, in the Friday update or sooner. Not the day it's due.
2. Say why in one sentence, give the new date, and say what it means for the invoice (SOP-13 finish-date rule). Apologise once, plainly.
3. Write the new date in the job. Don't move it twice; if you have to, call rather than email.

**A client is unhappy**
1. Reply the same day, even if only to say you've read it and will call tomorrow at a set time.
2. Call. Listen first. Repeat back what the problem is until they say "yes, that's it".
3. If you got it wrong: say so, fix it free, and say when it'll be fixed. If they've misunderstood the scope: show the list on the website and the agreement, kindly, and offer the extra at $150/hr or as a swap.
4. Write down what happened and what you'd change in the SOP. Change the SOP.

**A site is broken (yours or the client's)**
- Client's site down after your change: revert first, diagnose second. Tell the client. This is priority over everything, including sales.
- Client's profile suspended by Google: stop editing, read the suspension reason, gather documents (ABN, utility bill, signage photo), lodge the reinstatement with the client. Tell them the honest timeline (days to weeks). Don't create a new profile.

**A Fix-Up turns out unfixable**
- Stop at plan stage. Say so in writing with the reasons. Apply the $490 rule. Offer New Website with the credit. Never patch a site you know is beyond saving just to finish the list.

**You get sick or can't work**
- Tell every active client within a day with a realistic new date. Move monthly lists, don't skip them. A week's silence costs more than a week's delay.

## SOP-20 Weekly and monthly business review

**Every Friday, 4pm, 15 minutes (after the Friday updates)**
1. Open the CRM dashboard. Any lead in "New lead" over 2 business days? Fix now.
2. Any task overdue? Do it or re-date it with a reason.
3. Any job with the finish date in the next 5 days and the list under 80%? Plan the week or move the date (SOP-19).
4. Any invoice past day 10? Send T14.
5. Money in this week. Hours worked. Write both down.
6. One line: what would you change about this week?

**First business day of each month, one hour**
1. Numbers for last month: leads by source, checks sent on time (%), check → booked (%), booked → paid (%), average first sale, jobs finished on the promised date (%), monthly clients and months stayed, revenue (one-off + monthly), reviews and referrals.
2. Compare to the month before. Two lines on what moved and why.
3. Capacity next month: hours committed vs available. Decide how many new jobs you can take.
4. One SOP to improve, based on what went wrong or slow last month. Edit it. Update the CRM checklist to match.
5. Your own marketing (SOP-18): did the weekly things happen? Plan next month's.
6. Bookkeeping: set aside GST and tax, review subscriptions.
