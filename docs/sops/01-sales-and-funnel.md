# 01 — Sales and funnel

## SOP-01 Lead handling

**When:** any form on the site is submitted, or someone calls, emails or messages.

**Target:** a human reply within one business day. Free check sent within two business days.

**Steps**
1. The system creates the contact and lead, records the source, and sends the auto-reply (template T1). If the lead came by phone or in person, create it yourself in the CRM the same day, source "Phone" or "In person".
2. Read what they wrote. Check the business exists (Google their name). Check they're a local service business in Australia (anywhere; the work is remote).
3. Decide in 60 seconds: **Fit** (local service business, wins work by phone or booking) or **Not a fit** (online shop, national brand, wants ads or social, outside Australia).
4. Not a fit → send T15 (polite no, with a pointer to where they should go). Stage → Lost, reason recorded. Done.
5. Fit → the "Free check due" task is already there with a date. If they've asked a direct question, answer it now in one or two lines; don't wait for the check.
6. If they booked a package straight away (Tune-Up, Fix-Up, New Website, Monthly form) → go to SOP-04. Still do a quick check first so you know what you're selling.

**Done when:** they've had a human reply, the stage is right, and the next task has a date.

**Don't:** call a lead who didn't leave a number or ask for a call. Don't send a proposal. There are no proposals; there are prices.

## SOP-02 Free Local Search Check

**When:** task "Free check due" is on your list. Time box: 30–40 minutes.

**Steps**
1. Open the Free Check Builder in the CRM for that contact. It saves as you go.
2. **Pick five searches** a real customer would type. Use their "what jobs do you want more of" answer. Mix: two service + suburb ("hot water repair Burleigh Heads"), one "near me" style (check from their suburb using a location-set browser or a local rank tool), one broader ("plumber Gold Coast"), one question ("who's a good plumber in Burleigh").
3. **Google search:** for each, note whether they appear in the top results, the map pack, or not at all. Note which competitors do.
4. **Google Maps:** search their main service + suburb in Maps. Note their position if visible. Open their profile and score the gaps checklist in the builder: categories, services listed, service area, hours, description, photo count and quality, Q&A, reviews (count, recency, replies), duplicates, name/phone matches website.
5. **One AI tool:** ask ChatGPT (or Gemini) the question-style search. Record: named / mentioned / not named. Record who was named. Say nothing about why; the free check just reports it.
6. **Website (if any), three minutes only:** does it load fast on a phone, does the homepage say what they do and where in one line, is the phone tap-to-call, is there one page per main service. Note the three biggest gaps.
7. **Write the three fixes.** Plain words. Each one: what's wrong, what it costs them, what fixing it does. No jargon. Example: "Your profile lists one category, 'Plumber'. Google can't tell you do hot water, so you won't appear for hot water searches. Adding the right categories and services fixes that."
8. **Recommend one thing.** Tune-Up, Fix-Up, New Website, Monthly, or "you're in good shape, do nothing for now." Say why in one sentence. If two could fit, recommend the cheaper.
9. Generate the email (T2). Read it once out loud. Cut anything you stumbled on. Send from hello@takeoffseo.com.
10. Mark sent in the CRM. Stage → Check sent. Follow-up task appears automatically (+7 days).

**Done when:** email sent within two business days of the form, saved in the CRM, follow-up dated.

**Quality bar:** the owner should be able to forward the email to their partner and have them understand it without you.

## SOP-03 Follow-up and nurture

**When:** the follow-up tasks come due. Three touches maximum, then quarterly.

**Steps**
1. **Day 7 (T3):** "Did the check make sense? Anything you'd like me to explain?" One question, one line. Reply to your own check email so it's threaded.
2. **Day 30 (T4):** one useful thing, not a pitch. Something you noticed since, or a short plain tip for their trade. End with the price of the package you recommended and a link.
3. **No reply after that:** stage → Dormant. The system sets a quarterly check-in (T5): two lines, "still here, prices still on the site, no pressure".
4. **Any reply at any point:** answer within one business day. If they ask for a call, send the booking link. If they say "not now", ask "when should I check back?" and set the task for that date.
5. **They say no:** stage → Lost, reason recorded. Thank them. Never argue.

**Done when:** every lead has a dated next task or is in Lost.

**Don't:** send anything you'd be annoyed to receive. Don't add anyone to a newsletter; there isn't one.

## SOP-04 Booking a package

**When:** a package form is submitted, or someone says yes by phone or email.

**Steps**
1. Confirm the package fits. If they've booked a Fix-Up and the site is clearly beyond fixing, say so before the invoice: "You'd be better off with a New Website. Here's why." Same the other way: if a Tune-Up would do, say so. Recommending the cheaper option when it'll work is how you get referrals.
2. Work out the finish date (SOP-15). Write it down.
3. Send T6: booking confirmation, one-page agreement (in `07-templates.md`), and the invoice (deposit or full). Include the finish date and the "if I miss it, the second half waits" line.
4. Create the job in the CRM from the package template. The checklist appears. Stage → Booked.
5. Add the access checklist task for onboarding (SOP-05) so you can start the moment payment lands.
6. Payment received → stage → Active. Send T7 (onboarding). Start.
7. No payment after 5 business days → one friendly nudge (T14 variant). After 14 days → ask if they'd like to hold the spot or release it. Release it after 21 days; stage → Dormant.

**Done when:** agreement sent, invoice sent, finish date recorded, job and checklist exist.

**Don't:** start work before the deposit is in. Don't negotiate the price. If someone genuinely can't afford it, point to the smaller package.
